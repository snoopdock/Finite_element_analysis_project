\section{Introduction and Scope of the Finite Element Method}

The Finite Element Method (FEM) relies on the weak formulation to transform partial differential equations (PDEs) into integral equations, enabling the use of piecewise continuous basis functions and approximation of solutions on complex geometries. This approach is critical for deriving the discrete system of equations. The first step in FEM is to discretize the computational domain into a mesh of non-overlapping elements, which significantly impacts the accuracy and efficiency of the solution. Discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods, a class of FEM, allow solutions to be discontinuous across element boundaries, offering flexibility in mesh design and handling adaptive refinement efficiently. The DG solution satisfies the equation \$ \textbackslash{}sum\_\{K\} \textbackslash{}left( \textbackslash{}int\_K \textbackslash{}partial\_t u\_h v\_h dx - \textbackslash{}int\_K \textbackslash{}nabla u\_h \textbackslash{}cdot \textbackslash{}nabla v\_h dx + \textbackslash{}int\_\{\textbackslash{}partial K\} \textbackslash{}hat\{u\}\_h v\_h ds \textbackslash{}right) = \textbackslash{}int\_\{\textbackslash{}Omega\} f v\_h dx \$ for all \$ v\_h \textbackslash{}in V\_h \$.

The weak formulation is a foundational concept in the Finite Element Method (FEM) that transforms partial differential equations (PDEs) into integral equations by multiplying the PDE with a test function and integrating over the domain. This approach reduces the requirement for solution differentiability, allowing for the use of piecewise continuous basis functions and enabling the approximation of solutions on complex geometries. The weak form is critical for deriving the discrete system of equations, which is then solved iteratively or directly to obtain the discrete solution. According to [arxiv\_1209.2311v1], the weak formulation is essential for the development of FEM, and it has been widely used in various applications, including structural mechanics and fluid dynamics.

The Finite Element Limit Analysis (FELA) is an optimization-based method that computes upper or lower bound plastic collapse loads for mechanical systems by formulating the problem as an optimization task. According to the optimization objective function, the goal is to minimize the cost vector \$\textbackslash{}mathbf\{c\}\textasciicircum{}T \textbackslash{}mathbf\{\textbackslash{}sigma\}\$ while satisfying physical laws, as represented by the equation \$\textbackslash{}min\_\{\textbackslash{}mathbf\{\textbackslash{}sigma\}\} \textbackslash{}mathbf\{c\}\textasciicircum{}T \textbackslash{}mathbf\{\textbackslash{}sigma\} \textbackslash{}quad \textbackslash{}text\{subject to\} \textbackslash{}quad \textbackslash{}mathbf\{A\}\textbackslash{}mathbf\{\textbackslash{}sigma\} = \textbackslash{}mathbf\{b\}\$ [wiki\_finite\_element\_limit\_analysis]. This approach avoids time-stepping by directly determining collapse loads, ensuring efficiency and accuracy for geotechnical stability problems [wiki\_finite\_element\_limit\_analysis]. The Loubignac iteration, an iterative finite element method, is also employed to refine stress fields and achieve continuity, which is critical for improving the reliability of stress predictions in complex mechanical systems [wiki\_loubignac\_iteration].
