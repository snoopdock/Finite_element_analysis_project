The weak form is critical for deriving the discrete system of equations, which is obtained by multiplying the PDE by a test function and integrating over the domain. This approach reduces the requirement for solution differentiability, allowing for the use of discontinuous Galerkin (DG) methods, a class of FEM that enables solutions to be discontinuous across element boundaries. According to [arxiv\_1209.2311v1], DG methods use piecewise polynomial basis functions and enforce continuity weakly through numerical fluxes, making them particularly effective for problems with shocks, discontinuities, or high gradients.

Finite Element Limit Analysis (FELA) is an optimization-based method that computes upper or lower bound plastic collapse loads for mechanical systems by formulating the problem as an optimization task to compute collapse loads. According to the optimization objective function, the goal is to minimize the objective function while satisfying physical laws, represented by the equation \textbackslash{}min\_\{\textbackslash{}mathbf\{\textbackslash{}sigma\}\} \textbackslash{}mathbf\{c\}\textasciicircum{}T \textbackslash{}mathbf\{\textbackslash{}sigma\} \textbackslash{}quad \textbackslash{}text\{subject to\} \textbackslash{}quad \textbackslash{}mathbf\{A\}\textbackslash{}mathbf\{\textbackslash{}sigma\} = \textbackslash{}mathbf\{b\}. This method avoids time-stepping by directly determining collapse loads, leveraging mathematical programming techniques to ensure stability and accuracy in geotechnical applications, as stated in [wiki\_finite\_element\_limit\_analysis].

Discontinuous Galerkin Methods are a class of Finite Element Methods that allow solutions to be discontinuous across element boundaries, and they use piecewise polynomial basis functions and enforce continuity weakly through the use of stabilization terms. This approach is particularly effective for problems with shocks, discontinuities, or high gradients, as it offers flexibility in mesh design and can handle adaptive refinement efficiently.
