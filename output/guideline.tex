The variational equation, \(a(u, v) = L(v)\), represents the weak form of the partial differential equation, where \(u\) is the solution in the Hilbert space \(V\), \(v\) is a test function, and \(L(v)\) is a bounded linear functional. This equation forms the basis for the finite element discretization. The properties of the bilinear form \(a\) are crucial in establishing the validity of the variational equation. Specifically, the coercivity of the bilinear form, \(a(v, v) \geq \alpha \|v\|^2\), ensures the existence and uniqueness of the solution to the variational problem. Furthermore, the continuity of the bilinear form, \(|a(v, w)| \leq \gamma \|v\| \|w\|\), guarantees that the bilinear form does not grow unbounded with respect to the input functions. These properties are essential in applying Céa's lemma, which provides a framework for proving error estimates in the finite element method.

The continuity and coercivity of bilinear forms are essential properties in establishing the well-posedness of the variational equation. On the other hand, the coercivity of the bilinear form \(a\) is enforced by the inequality \(a(v, v) \geq \alpha \|v\|^2\) \cite{src:wiki_c_a_s_lemma-c997aed28ad6}, where \(\alpha\) is a positive constant. These properties, in conjunction with Céa's lemma \cite{src:wiki_c_a_s_lemma-c997aed28ad6}, provide a framework for proving error estimates and ensuring the convergence of the finite element solution to the true solution under appropriate conditions.
