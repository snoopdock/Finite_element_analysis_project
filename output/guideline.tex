\section{The Finite Element Procedure}
\label{sec:a4b564da-7564-46bb-8701-55078bd44cec}

The Finite Element Method (FEM) relies on the discretization of a domain into smaller, simpler parts called finite elements. This process, denoted as \(\Omega \approx \bigcup_{e=1}^{N} \Omega_e\), involves defining nodes, elements, and interpolation functions to approximate the solution. Discretization facilitates the handling of complex geometries and boundary conditions by breaking them into manageable parts, enabling the modeling of intricate physical phenomena. By dividing the domain into finite elements, FEM approximates solutions using piecewise functions, which is essential for solving partial differential equations.

The Weak Form of Poisson's Equation plays a pivotal role in transforming the governing partial differential equation into a weak form, thereby facilitating the use of piecewise functions in the Finite Element Method (FEM). The resulting weak form reduces the smoothness requirements for the solution, enabling the use of numerical methods to approximate the solution. This process is essential for deriving the discrete equations in FEM, ensuring compatibility with numerical methods.

The process of Domain Discretization is a foundational step in the Finite Element Method (FEM), where the physical domain is divided into a mesh of finite elements. This involves selecting appropriate element types (e.g., triangles, quadrilaterals) and ensuring sufficient refinement in regions of interest, as the mesh must conform to the geometry and capture relevant features. Discretization is essential, as it determines the approximation space for the solution, allowing numerical solutions to be computed by defining nodes, elements, and interpolation functions to approximate the solution. The accuracy of FEM results depends heavily on the quality and density of the mesh, which is a critical aspect of the discretization process.
