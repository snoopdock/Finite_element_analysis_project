\section{Mathematical Foundation: Strong Form, Weak Form, and Galerkin Method}
\label{sec:7990454e-9eb1-4fcb-a324-80667fcf2812}

The formulation of a problem in the context of finite element limit analysis (FELA) involves defining the physical problem, including geometry, material properties, boundary conditions, and loading scenarios. The domain is then discretized into finite elements, ensuring appropriate mesh density for accuracy. For FELA, the problem is formulated as an optimization task, where the goal is to directly compute collapse loads without time-stepping. This is achieved by solving the optimization problem using algorithms tailored to the formulation, such as second-order cone programming for nonlinear optimization. The optimization problem can be represented by the equation \(\min_{\mathbf{x}} \mathbf{c}^T \mathbf{x} \quad \text{subject to} \quad \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}, \quad \mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{K}\), where \(\mathbf{x}\) is the vector of variables, \(\mathbf{c}\) is the cost coefficient, \(\mathbf{A}\) and \(\mathbf{b}\) define equality constraints, and \(\mathcal{K}\) denotes the second-order cone.

The Galerkin method, a cornerstone of finite element limit analysis (FELA), relies on optimization techniques to determine upper or lower bound plastic collapse loads. In the context of curved elements in Weak Galerkin Finite Element Methods (WG-FEM), the use of these elements enables accurate modeling of complex geometries by approximating curved boundaries, thereby enhancing the flexibility of WG-FEM for problems with irregular domains \cite{ref4}. The optimization problem in FELA is formulated as a second-order cone programming (SOCP) problem, which ensures the solution adheres to plasticity and stability conditions: \(\min_{\mathbf{x}} \mathbf{c}^T \mathbf{x} \quad \text{subject to} \quad \mathbf{A}\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{b}, \quad \mathbf{x} \in \mathcal{K}\) \cite{ref1}.

The incorporation of curved elements in Weak Galerkin Finite Element Methods (WG-FEM) is crucial for maintaining the accuracy of complex geometries. As stated in \cite{ref4}, curved elements enable the approximation of curved boundaries, thereby enhancing the flexibility of WG-FEM for problems with irregular domains. This is particularly important in applications requiring high-fidelity geometric representation. Furthermore, the use of curved elements reduces discretization errors, as emphasized in \cite{ref4}, which is essential for maintaining solution fidelity in problems with irregular boundaries or nonlinear deformations. For FELA, a choice between kinematic (velocity-based) or equilibrium (stress-based) formulations is necessary. Solving the discretized problem in Finite Element Limit Analysis (FELA) involves employing optimization algorithms tailored to the formulation. For kinematic FELA, the goal is to minimize the dissipated energy subject to kinematic constraints, whereas for equilibrium FELA, the objective is to maximize the load factor while satisfying stress equilibrium. Optimization solvers such as second-order cone programming can be employed for nonlinear optimization, iterating until convergence criteria are met.
