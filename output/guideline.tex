\label{sec:49477b52-afde-48e5-a277-065c31facd86}

Galerkin's method is a foundational technique in the Finite Element Method (FEM) that transforms partial differential equations into a weak form by converting the governing partial differential equations into a weak (variational) form. This is achieved by multiplying the PDE by a test function and integrating over the domain, which reduces the smoothness requirements for the solution and enables the use of piecewise basis functions. The weak form, as represented by the equation \(\int_{\Omega} \nabla u_h \cdot \nabla v \, d\Omega = \int_{\Omega} f v \, d\Omega\), ensures that the solution satisfies the governing equations in an integral sense rather than pointwise, making it a critical step for handling complex boundary conditions and nonlinear problems.

Converting the strong form of partial differential equations (PDEs) into a weak form using test functions is a crucial step in the Finite Element Method (FEM). This process involves multiplying the PDE by a test function and integrating over the domain, which accommodates discontinuous fields and natural boundary conditions. For mixed methods, additional variables such as pressure or flux are introduced into the weak form. The weak form is essential for applying Galerkin's method and ensuring numerical stability, as it reduces the smoothness requirements for the solution, making it easier to approximate using piecewise functions.
