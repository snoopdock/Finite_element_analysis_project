Galerkin's method is a foundational technique in the Finite Element Method (FEM) that transforms partial differential equations into a weak form. This is achieved by formulating the weak form of the governing equations, which involves multiplying the PDE by a test function and integrating over the domain. The weak form is essential for applying Galerkin's method and ensuring numerical stability. The weak form of a PDE is defined as \(\int_{\Omega} \delta u \cdot f \, d\Omega = 0\), where \(\delta u\) is a test function and \(f\) represents the residual of the PDE. This approach ensures that the solution satisfies the governing equations in an integral sense rather than pointwise. The method is critical for deriving variational formulations and is widely used in structural and electromagnetic simulations.
