The finite element method relies on the properties of the bilinear form in the variational equation, and Céa's Lemma provides a framework for proving error estimates by relating the exact solution of a variational problem to its finite-dimensional approximation \cite{src:wiki_c_a_s_lemma-c997aed28ad6}.

The weak formulation plays a pivotal role in ensuring the existence and uniqueness of the weak solution in the finite element method. This formulation transforms a partial differential equation into an integral form by multiplying with a test function and integrating over the domain, thereby relaxing the differentiability requirements of the solution. The coercivity of the bilinear form, \(a(v, v) \geq \alpha \|v\|^2\) \cite{src:wiki_c_a_s_lemma-c997aed28ad6}, guarantees the existence and uniqueness of the weak solution, as it ensures that the form is bounded below by a positive multiple of the norm squared of the input function. This property is critical for the stability of numerical methods and is a key condition in the Babuška–Lax–Milgram theorem \cite{src:wiki_babu_ka_lax_milgram_theorem-a334b87f26cc}.

The Spectral Element Method (SEM) presents an attractive alternative to traditional Finite Element Methods (FEM) for solving partial differential equations. By combining features of spectral methods and finite elements, SEM employs high-degree polynomial basis functions to achieve exponential convergence rates with fewer degrees of freedom. This is particularly beneficial for problems requiring high precision, such as wave propagation, where SEM can mitigate the need for fine meshes by using high-degree polynomials, thus reducing computational time \cite{src:wiki_spectral_element_method-55f1a6279df2}. However, SEM faces challenges in modeling complex geometries due to its reliance on structured, high-order elements, which may complicate mesh generation. The coercivity of a bilinear form, a critical property in ensuring the existence and uniqueness of the weak solution, is also essential for SEM to guarantee the stability of the numerical method \cite{src:wiki_babu_ka_lax_milgram_theorem-a334b87f26cc}.
